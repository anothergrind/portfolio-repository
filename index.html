About

KO

I'm a machine learning student focused on time-series and rare-event prediction. Space weather is what pulled me in: geomagnetic storms can disrupt satellites, GPS, aviation, and power grids, but they're rare enough that a careless model can post a great accuracy score while catching almost nothing. Learning to tell those two situations apart — and to build evaluation that doesn't lie to you — is the part of ML I find most interesting.

Most of my recent work has gone into the Geomagnetic Storm Predictor, built with a six-person AI4ALL Ignite team: Nithila Sadheesh, Nancy Nakyung Kwak, Chan Li, Hafsah Khan, Nana Oppong Ampofo, and me. We combined OMNI solar-wind, NASA DONKI CME, solar-flare, and geomagnetic-index sources into an 87,649-row dataset binned into 3-hour windows, trained both tabular and sequence classifiers using chronological splits to avoid time leakage, and deployed all three final models behind an interactive Streamlit dashboard. Next I want to go deeper on sequence models and probability calibration.

Projects

All three projects below are the deployed classifiers from the Geomagnetic Storm Predictor, a six-person AI4ALL Ignite team project. Each one predicts whether the Ap index will reach 50 — roughly Kp 5 storm conditions — in the next 3 hours, and all three are trained on 2010–2021 and scored on the same held-out 2022–2024 test period, so the numbers are directly comparable.

Storm Predictor — XGBoost

A gradient-boosted tree classifier that forecasts storm risk 3 hours ahead from 45 engineered features of the current 3-hour bin — solar wind, magnetic field, flare counts, CME activity, and 27-day recurrence — with no current Ap features. Highest recall of the three, and the most interpretable baseline.

DatasetSingle 3-hour bins, 2010–2024 (OMNI + DONKI + flares)
ModelXGBoost + RandomOverSampler, threshold 0.90
ResultPR-AUC 0.575 · Recall 0.684 · F1 0.485

Storm Predictor — LSTM

A 2×128 recurrent sequence model trained with pos-weighted BCE that reads a rolling 48-hour window of 8 solar-wind and Ap channels instead of a single bin. Best precision and PR-AUC of the three — when it calls a storm, it's usually right.

Dataset48-hour sequences (16 bins × 8 channels), 2010–2024
ModelLSTM 2×128, pos-weighted BCE, threshold 0.795
ResultPR-AUC 0.610 · Precision 0.729 · F1 0.555

Storm Predictor — TCN

A causal dilated convolutional network trained with focal loss on a rolling 48-hour solar-wind history, without ever seeing the current Ap index. Architecture and imbalance strategy were both chosen on a chronological validation split, then refit once on the full training window. Best overall F1 — the most balanced of the three.

Dataset48-hour solar-wind sequences, no current Ap, 2010–2024
ModelTCN 4×64, focal loss (α .75, γ 2), threshold 0.60
ResultF1 0.580 · PR-AUC 0.602 · ROC-AUC 0.956
